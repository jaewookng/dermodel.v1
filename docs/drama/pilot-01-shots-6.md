# THE NANNY — Pilot 01 · 6-shot cut
### ~70 seconds · Kling Video v3.0 Omni · 9:16

Re-cut from 11 shots to 6. Each shot now carries **exactly one beat**, so nothing
structural was lost — what went is the groceries/laundry montage (folded into
shot 2) and the stairs hesitation (folded into shot 6).

**Duration plan under a 15s/generation cap — total ~82s.**

Do NOT max every shot to 15s. Artifacting and identity drift compound with
clip duration, so spend the long clips where there is no face to drift:

| Shot | Length | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 12s | Two faces + three lines of dialogue. Long enough to play, short enough to hold. |
| 2 | 10s | Single face, no dialogue. Nothing to fill 15s with except drift. |
| 3 | 15s | Safe to max: Teddy faces away, Nora is at distance. Little exposed face. |
| 4 | 15s | Hands and a phone screen only. Zero drift risk. |
| 5 | 15s | Hands and a bottle only. Zero drift risk, and the reveal wants room. |
| 6 | 13s | Single face, the cliffhanger. Cut on her turn, do not linger. |

⚠️ **A 15s clip needs 15s of described action.** If you ask for 15 seconds and
describe a five-second beat, the model fills the remainder with repetition or
wandering. The three long shots below have been rewritten with more internal
beats for exactly this reason — use those versions, not the short ones.

**Two shots are deliberately hands-and-objects, not faces.** With only six
generations you cannot afford drift, and every second of visible face is a
chance for the model to wander. Shots 4 and 5 carry the most important story
information and the least facial risk. That is not a compromise — it is how you
survive a six-shot budget.

> ⚠️ **Generate 5 first, keep 1 in reserve.** With no re-roll budget, one bad
> output kills the episode. Hold shot 2 back — it is the most replaceable — and
> use the spare generation to fix whichever of the other five comes out worst.

**Bind the saved Element for:** shots 1, 2, 3, 6 (faces).
**No element needed for:** shots 4, 5 (hands, phone, bottles).
