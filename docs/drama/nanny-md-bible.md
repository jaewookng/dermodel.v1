# NANNY, MD — series bible
**Vertical 9:16 · ~90s episodes · YouTube Shorts**

> Title card: **NANNY, M̶D̶** — the credential struck through. She never finished.

---

## The load-bearing decision

She has a mental health condition. **It never makes her wrong.**

This is not a softening — it is the only version that works. The engine is *she
is right and nobody listens*. If her illness makes her unreliable, the audience
stops trusting her too, and the series has no spine. Worse, the show would be
quietly arguing that people with mental illness can't be trusted, which is both
false and the opposite of what this audience believes.

So: **her judgment is sound in every episode.** What her illness costs her is
*credibility with other people* — it is the lever they use to dismiss her. The
tension is not "is she reliable?" It is:

> She is right, no one will believe her, and she is beginning to doubt herself
> because everyone else does.

That is more painful, more true, and dramatically stronger.

**Handling rules.** Her condition is shown matter-of-factly — she takes her
medication, she has a routine, she manages it competently. It is never a twist,
never a reveal, never horror, and never depicted through self-harm. Showing
someone manage a condition well while the world treats her as fragile *is* the
argument.

---

## Backstory — why "disgraced"

Third year of medical school, clinical rotations. Nora flagged an error by a
senior attending. She was correct.

She was managed out. The paperwork says she took medical leave for her mental
health, and that part is true — the months after were genuinely bad. But the
sequence matters: **she was right first, and destroyed for it second.** The
official story is that she broke down. The real story is that she was
inconvenient, and her diagnosis was the most convenient way to file her.

She has never been able to tell those two versions apart in her own head. That
is the wound.

**Why this is the right backstory:** the pilot asks her to be right again, out
loud, in a house that has already decided what she is. The audience knows
exactly what that costs her. No exposition required.

---

## Engine

> She is the most qualified person in the room and the least believed.

Every episode her expertise surfaces something real. Every episode, speaking up
costs more than staying quiet. She is not licensed — she *cannot* act. She can
only get someone with authority to listen, and nobody will.

## Characters

**NORA VANCE, 22.** Med school, third year, not finished. Sharp, quiet,
watchful. Takes her medication each morning without ceremony. Anchors: grey knit
cardigan · tight low ponytail loosening through the day · thin silver watch,
left wrist · no makeup. New anchor: **a worn clinical handbook** she still keeps.

**ELEANOR MERCER, 48.** Employer. Knows about the medical leave — it is why she
felt safe hiring someone this overqualified, and why she is untroubled about
dismissing her. Anchors: cream silk blouse · fine gold chain · chestnut blowout ·
the ring she turns when lying.

**TEDDY MERCER, 14.** Intellectually disabled. Written through **behaviour, never
visual signifiers.** He keeps count of which nights his mother is home. He is the
only person in the house who takes Nora at her word — which is the relationship
the series is actually about. Anchors: navy hoodie · handheld puzzle toy · his
place at the window.

## PILOT — six beats, ~90s

**1 · Cold open (12s).** Kitchen. Eleanor, jaw and throat red and raised, serum
bottle in hand. *"You used them. Don't lie to me."* Nora: *"I never touched
them."* Eleanor, pleasantly: *"The nanny doesn't touch my things."*

**2 · The slight (10s).** Nora alone. She takes a pill from a daily organiser
with total routine — no music sting, no lingering, it is just Tuesday. Behind
her on the shelf: a worn clinical handbook. She puts the glass down and goes
back to work. *We understand her whole history in six seconds and no one says a
word about it.*

**3 · Teddy (15s).** He is at the window with the puzzle. *"Is Mom home
tonight?"* — *"…Not tonight."* — *"She was home Tuesday."* He is keeping count.
Nora looks at him properly for the first time in the episode.

**4 · The read (15s).** Her room. Hands only, no face. She turns the serum box
over, opens dermodel, the ingredient list scrolls, her thumb stops.
`Citrus Paradisi Peel Oil — found in 311 products`. Whispered: *"…grapefruit."*

**5 · Confirmation (15s).** Bathroom, hands and objects only. She lifts
Eleanor's second bottle into a hard band of afternoon sun. **Glycolic acid.**
She holds both bottles in the light and goes completely still. *A phototoxic
citrus oil on acid-stripped skin in direct sun. She was sent to buy it.*

**6 · Cliffhanger (13s).** Top of the stairs, a bottle in each hand, about to go
down and say so. She stops — she knows exactly what being right costs her.
Eleanor, warm, from below: *"And bring up Teddy's medication while you're
there."* Nora turns her head to the cabinet. **Cut to black.**

---

## Why beat 6 is the series

She has already looked in that cabinet. She has already noticed something. And
she has already decided, once before, what happens to her when she says so.

The audience assembles that in the last half-second. You never state the premise.

## Craft notes carried forward

- Beats 4 and 5 are **hands and objects, no face** — zero drift risk, and the
  strongest filmmaking in the episode.
- Cap continuous face time ~3s. Long clips need long described actions.
- Two locations. Three faces. Elements already saved — never re-describe a
  character in a shot prompt.
- **She is never shown practising medicine.** She has no licence. She can only
  know things and try to be heard — which is the engine.

## Guardrails

Fictional brands only (*Maison Lelan*). No before/after skin footage, ever. The
app informs, Nora deduces — never show it concluding. Placement ~6s, once,
randomised across episodes. AI disclosure ON at upload.
