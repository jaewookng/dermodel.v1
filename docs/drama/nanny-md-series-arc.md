# NANNY, MD — full series arc
**60 episodes × ~90s · four acts of 15 · vertical 9:16**

Sixty episodes is the licensing unit — ReelShort and DramaBox buy 60–90 episode
series outright. Published to YouTube Shorts in act-sized runs so each act is a
self-contained watch with a hard turn at its end.

---

## Logline

A third-year medical school dropout, filed away as "unwell" after she was right
about the wrong person, takes a live-in job caring for a wealthy family's
disabled son — and realises the boy is being quietly sedated into
convenience by the same kind of people who did it to her.

## Theme

> **Being managed is what happens to inconvenient people.**

Nora and Teddy are the same story at different ages. She was medicated into
silence by a system that found her disruptive; he is being medicated into
stillness by a family that finds him inconvenient. She is the only person who
recognises it, because it is the only thing she has ever been an expert in.

## Engine

> She is the most qualified person in the room and the least believed.

Every episode: she is right, saying so costs her, and staying quiet costs Teddy.

---

## THE SPINE

**What is actually happening.** Teddy is on a sedating antipsychotic prescribed
off-label by the family's concierge physician, escalating over two years. Not a
conspiracy — something far more ordinary. A private doctor who writes what
wealthy parents ask for, parents who wanted a calm child for the house they
entertain in, and a boy who cannot advocate for himself. Everyone involved
believes they are doing something reasonable.

**Why nobody stops it.** Every adult has a reason not to look. Eleanor needs the
house to run. Marcus is never home. Dr. Aldrich is paid handsomely to be
agreeable. The housekeeper has known for years and needs the job. Nora is the
only one with both the knowledge to see it and nothing left to lose — and the
only one whose word is worthless.

## CAST

| **TEDDY MERCER, 14** | Intellectually disabled. Keeps count of everything. The only person who takes Nora at her word. **Written through behaviour, never visual signifiers.** |

## ACT ONE — "The Cabinet" · Episodes 1–15

*She notices. Every small, safe attempt to do something fails.*

- **1.** The serum accusation. She is blamed, reads the label, and is right. Ends on Eleanor asking her to fetch Teddy's medication. *(pilot)*
- **2.** The cabinet. She reads the label properly. The dose is wrong for his weight and the drug is wrong for his diagnosis. She says nothing.
- **3–4.** She tries the smallest possible intervention — a mild question to Eleanor, framed as curiosity. Dismissed pleasantly. *"Dr. Aldrich handles all that."*
- **5.** Teddy has a good day. She sees who he is underneath it. This is the episode that makes the audience need her to win.
- **6–7.** She starts keeping notes. Times, doses, behaviour. Her old clinical handbook comes off the shelf.
- **8.** Mrs. Hall catches her looking and says the series' coldest line: *"I wouldn't."*
- **9–10.** Dr. Aldrich visits. He is charming, asks Nora what she studied, and something in the way he says *"ah"* tells her he already knows her file.
- **11–12.** Eleanor raises the medical leave for the first time — gently, as concern. The threat is unmistakable and entirely deniable.
- **13–14.** Teddy has a bad night. Nora manages it correctly and alone. Nobody thanks her; Eleanor is annoyed she was woken.
- **15.** **ACT TURN.** Nora finds an old prescription in a drawer — the original dose, from two years ago. It is a quarter of what he takes now. Someone has been escalating him steadily, and it is written in Aldrich's hand.

## ACT TWO — "Chart Review" · Episodes 16–30

*She builds a case. She acquires an ally. She is seen.*

- **16–17.** She contacts Priya. Their first conversation is not warm — Priya was there when Nora fell apart and has her own version of it.
- **18–20.** Priya, reluctantly, pulls what she can. The drug, the dose, the trajectory. Confirms it: this is sedation, not treatment.
- **21.** Teddy shows Nora his puzzle. He has been solving it the same way for a year — since the dose went up. **He used to solve it faster.** He knows something is wrong with him and has no words for it.
- **22–24.** Nora starts documenting properly. Photographs, timestamps, a spreadsheet. The show gets procedural and the audience gets to be ahead of the room.
- **25–26.** Marcus comes home. He is kind to Nora, kind to Teddy, and does not notice anything. She realises kindness is not the same as attention.
- **27–28.** Aldrich adjusts the dose upward again after a "difficult week" that Nora knows was caused by something else entirely.
- **29.** Nora nearly tells Marcus. She gets three sentences in before Eleanor enters the room. The look Eleanor gives her ends Act Two.
- **30.** **ACT TURN.** Eleanor searches Nora's room while she is out and finds the notes. She does not confront her. She simply schedules an appointment — for Nora — with Dr. Aldrich.

---

## ACT THREE — "Unwell" · Episodes 31–45

*Her history is turned into a weapon. She loses.*

- **31–32.** The appointment. Aldrich is gentle, thorough, and utterly professional as he builds a record of a young woman fixating on a family's private medical affairs.
- **33–35.** Eleanor's campaign is soft and total: concerned remarks to Marcus, a call to the agency, a suggestion that Nora seems tired. Nothing is a lie. Everything is a knife.
- **36.** Nora doubts herself for the first time on screen. She re-checks her own notes three times. **They are correct.** The audience must see they are correct.
- **37–38.** Priya pulls back — she has a career, and Nora is asking her to risk it on a hunch about a family she has never met.
- **39–40.** Teddy deteriorates visibly. Nora is the only one who names it, and naming it is now proof she is obsessed.
- **41–42.** Mrs. Hall tells Nora what happened to the last caregiver who asked questions. It is not dramatic. She was simply let go, and nobody ever heard from her again.
- **43–44.** Nora is dismissed. She packs in one shot. Teddy is not allowed to say goodbye.
- **45.** **ACT TURN — the lowest point.** On the train home she opens her notes to delete them. Then she reads the last line she wrote, in Teddy's words: *"She was home Tuesday."* He counts because nobody else does. **She turns around.**

---

## ACT FOUR — "Attending" · Episodes 46–60

*Not heroics. She gets one person with authority to listen.*

- **46–48.** She cannot enter the house, cannot access the records, has no standing. She has notes and no credibility — the exact position she has been in since she was twenty.
- **49–50.** She goes to Priya one last time. Not asking her to risk anything: asking her *who to send it to.* That distinction is the whole episode.
- **51–53.** She builds a report. Properly. Clinical, sourced, unemotional — the work of the doctor she nearly was. This is the series' quiet triumph and it happens at a kitchen table.
- **54–55.** Marcus reads it. The scene is not a confrontation; it is a man realising he has been absent from his own son's life in a way he cannot undo.
- **56–57.** Aldrich is reviewed. He is not destroyed — men like him rarely are — but he is removed from the family. The realistic outcome lands harder than a comeuppance.
- **58.** Teddy's regimen is tapered under proper supervision. **He solves the puzzle faster.** No dialogue. This is the emotional payoff of the entire series.
- **59.** Eleanor and Nora, one last scene. Eleanor does not apologise. She says something almost worse: *"You were very good with him."* Past tense. She still cannot say the other thing.
- **60.** **SERIES TURN.** A letter arrives — the medical school will hear an appeal. Nora sits with it unopened, and we understand she is not afraid of failing. She is afraid of being believed and then having to become the person she stopped being.
  **Final shot:** she opens it. Cut to black. **Season two.**

## EPISODE TEMPLATE

Every episode, six beats, ~90s:

1. **Cold open, mid-conflict.** A line, never a setup.
2. **The slight.** Her expertise is dismissed, discounted, or unasked.
3. **Teddy.** He notices something nobody else does.
4. **The read.** She examines something — a label, a chart, a dose, a date.
5. **Confirmation.** She is right. Hands and objects, no faces.
6. **The cost.** Being right makes her position worse. Cliffhanger.

Beats 4 and 5 are always hands-and-objects. Zero drift risk, cheapest to
generate, and the best filmmaking in the show.

## PLACEMENT PLAN

Dermodel appears in roughly **one episode in three**, never more than ~6
seconds, always as a reference she consults — never as a source of conclusions.
Natural homes across the arc:

- **Act 1:** the serum reveal (ep 1), a cosmetic ingredient interaction in
  Eleanor's rotation (ep 6), a product Teddy reacts to (ep 12)
- **Act 2:** cross-referencing an excipient in his formulation (ep 19),
  a topical Aldrich recommends (ep 27)
- **Act 3:** she checks something for herself, at 3am, alone (ep 36) — the
  episode where she doubts herself and the tool tells her she is right
- **Act 4:** absent entirely. By the endgame she is working from clinical
  sources. **Removing it at the climax is what keeps it from reading as an ad.**

The rule never changes: **the app informs, Nora deduces.**

## GUARDRAILS

- **Her condition never makes her wrong.** Non-negotiable. It costs her
  credibility with others, never her judgment.
- **No self-harm depiction.** No reveal-twist framing. Her management of her
  condition is routine and competent.
- **Teddy is a person, not a cause.** He notices, he counts, he has preferences,
  he is funny. Never written through visual signifiers of disability.
- **Nora never practises medicine.** No licence. She can only know, and try to
  be heard. That limitation *is* the engine.
- **Fictional everything medical.** Invented drug names, invented brands
  (*Maison Lelan*). Real pharmacology, no real product ever named as harmful.
- **No before/after skin or health footage.**
- **AI disclosure ON** at every upload.
