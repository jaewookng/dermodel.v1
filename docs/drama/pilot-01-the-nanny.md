# THE NANNY — Pilot 01
### 90s vertical · YouTube Shorts · AI-generated (disclosed)

**Logline.** A biochemistry graduate takes a live-in caregiving job with a wealthy
couple and finds she was hired as staff, not as help — and that the only person
in the house who notices anything is the boy she was supposed to be caring for.

**Engine.** *She is right, and nobody listens.* Every episode she reads something
correctly — a label, a chart, a silence — and is punished for it. The audience
gets the satisfaction of being ahead of the room with her.

**Tone.** Cold, quiet, controlled. No score under dialogue. The house is
beautiful and hostile. Think *Parasite* domestic tension, not soap.

**Placement rule.** Dermodel appears for **6 seconds, once**, and only ever
*informs*. Nora does the deducing — she is the chemist, the app is the reference.
The product never diagnoses, warns, or concludes. This keeps the claim honest and
keeps her competent.

## CAST — reference sheet (lock these into Veo / Kling before generating)

**NORA VANCE — 22.** Biochemistry BSc. Pale, light freckles, dark blonde hair
pulled back tight and slightly unravelling by evening. Grey knit cardigan over a
plain white tee, dark jeans, no jewellery, no makeup. Reads as competent and
underdressed for the house. *Consistency anchors: the cardigan, the tight-then-
loosening hair, a thin silver watch on the left wrist.*

**ELEANOR MERCER — 48.** The wife. Silk blouse, blowout, gold at the throat.
Beautiful and rehearsed. Speaks warmly while saying cruel things. *Anchors: the
gold necklace, the blouse, a wedding ring she turns when lying.*

**TEDDY MERCER — 14.** Intellectually disabled. He is not a symptom — **he is
the only other observer in the house**, and he notices things before the adults.
Watchful, literal, funny when he wants to be. Plays with a handheld puzzle.
*Anchors: navy hoodie, the puzzle toy, his spot at the window.*

**Never shown in Pilot 01:** the husband. He is referenced and absent. That
absence is the point.

## WHY THIS ENDING

The same skill that just exonerated her is about to be pointed at the boy's
medication. The audience makes that leap themselves in the last half-second —
which is what earns the swipe to Episode 2. You never state the series premise;
you hand them the shape of it.

---

## PRODUCTION NOTES

- **11 shots.** Two locations, three faces, one of whom (Teddy) is in two shots.
  Sized deliberately for character-drift control.
- **Longest continuous face shot: 3s.** Drift compounds with duration — cut before
  the model wanders.
- **No skin-result footage, ever.** Eleanor's rash is shown in the cold open as
  irritation, never as a before/after. Implied results are the FTC's active
  enforcement area and this genre does not need them.
- **Fictional brand** — *Maison Lelan*. The chemistry is real; the product is not.
  Naming a real serum and dramatizing that it caused a rash is trade libel.
- **AI disclosure toggle ON** at upload. Required, and costs nothing in this genre.

## THE CHEMISTRY IS REAL

Citrus peel oils contain **furocoumarins**, which are phototoxic — they produce a
delayed rash on sun-exposed skin (phytophotodermatitis). Layered over skin
freshly exfoliated with an AHA, in direct light, that reaction is exactly what a
biochemist would predict on sight.

Pulled from the live database: a real "caviar" vitamin C serum lists
`Citrus Paradisi Peel Oil` at position 4, and the ingredient appears in **311
products**. Nora's read is correct. That is the whole point — the show can be
checked, and it holds up.
